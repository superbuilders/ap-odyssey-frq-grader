What you earned — level 3
“Provides specific evidence to support all claims in a line of reasoning. Explains how some of the evidence supports a line of reasoning. AND Explains how at least one rhetorical choice contributes to the writer's argument, purpose, or message.”
What level 4 adds
“Consistently explains how the evidence supports a line of reasoning. AND Explains how multiple rhetorical choices contribute to the writer's argument, purpose, or message.” The evidence requirement is identical at 3 and 4. Only the commentary changes.
In your essay
“…tying Reagan's name to the idea itself, so that an attack on the man becomes an attack on the thing he stood for.” A choice, tied to what it accomplishes. This is the one that earned level 3.
“By calling him a man who ‘sought to mend’… she reframes his hardest Cold War decisions as healing rather than aggression.” You named the move and paraphrased it — but never said what it does for her argument. The sentence stops one clause early.
To earn the fourth point

Finish the “sought to mend” sentence. You said it reframes him as healing — now say why that matters to her case: it lets her defend the hard decisions without ever having to argue for them. Two explained choices instead of one is the whole difference between 3 and 4.

In your essay
“This disarms a reader who came in skeptical of him.” You identified the audience and their resistance — that is the hard half of path one. But you stopped at the observation. The point needs the situation: why this audience, at this moment, made that choice necessary.
Your closest path

One sentence after “…skeptical of him.” In 2004 Thatcher is eulogising a man much of her audience still blames for the Cold War's hardest years. Say that, and the softening stops being a nice touch and becomes a necessary one. That's the sophistication point.
